The Capacitated Facility Location Problem aims to determine the optimal subset of facilities to open from a set of $m$ potential candidate locations to satisfy the deterministic demands of a set of $n$ distributed customers. Each potential facility $i$ is characterized by a fixed opening cost $f_i$ and a maximum supply capacity $C_i$. Each customer $j$ has a predefined demand $d_j$ that must be fully completely met.
A facility can only supply products to customers if it is explicitly chosen to be opened, thereby incurring its fixed opening cost. Once an opened facility begins operations, the total amount of goods shipped from facility $i$ to all customers must not exceed its capacity $C_i$. Shipping one unit of product from facility $i$ to customer $j$ incurs a deterministic transportation cost $c_{ij}$. A customer's demand can be satisfied by a single opened facility or fractionally fulfilled by multiple opened facilities. The location-allocation decision involves determining exactly which facilities to open and the exact shipment quantities from each facility to each customer, ensuring that all demands are met without violating any capacity constraints.
The objective is to minimize the total system cost, defined as the sum of the fixed costs for opening facilities and the total variable transportation costs for delivering goods to customers.
