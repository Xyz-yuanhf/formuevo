The Quadratic Assignment Problem aims to determine the optimal one-to-one assignment of a set of $n$ facilities to a set of $n$ distinct locations. Each facility must be assigned to exactly one location, and each location must be occupied by exactly one facility, establishing a strict bijective mapping.
For the given problem data, there is a deterministic flow $f_{i,j}$ (e.g., weight, material, or traffic) between every pair of facilities $i$ and $j$, and a deterministic distance $d_{k,l}$ between every pair of locations $k$ and $l$. The decision involves determining the exact spatial mapping of all facilities to the locations. The underlying complexity of the problem stems from its cost structure: the cost incurred by assigning facility $i$ to location $k$ and facility $j$ to location $l$ is strictly defined as the product of their inter-facility flow and their inter-location distance (i.e., $f_{i,j} \times d_{k,l}$). 
The objective is to minimize the total assignment cost, calculated as the sum of all flow-distance products across every pair of facilities. Since the formulation inherently involves the product of assignment decisions, the model requires careful structural design and proper linearization techniques to be solved efficiently as a mathematically rigorous Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) model.
